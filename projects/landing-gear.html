Automated Landing Gear for a Falcon 9-type Rocket

MechatronicsCADControl

Summary

Inspired by the reusable Falcon 9 booster, our student association worked on a rocket that lands on its own legs. I designed the retractable legs in SolidWorks and programmed the deployment sequence and the descent control: legs stowed during ascent, deployed below a set altitude, locked at touchdown.

On the bench

A small-scale version of the problems of real ride and robotic systems: a mechanism that must deploy at the right moment every time, an actuator sized for the load, sensors read in real time and a sequence that falls back to a safe state.

Key results

  • Retractable legs designed in SolidWorks
  • Deployment sequence and descent control programmed

Method & equations

The deployment is a state machine driven by the measured altitude \(h(t)\): ascent → descent → deploy when \(h$$\tau_{\text{act}}\geq m\,g\,\ell\,\sin\theta,\qquad \theta\in[0,\theta_{\max}]$$

The descent is regulated by feedback around a reference speed profile, for example with a PID-type law:

$$u(t)=K_p\,e(t)+K_i\int_0^t e\,d\tau+K_d\,\dot e(t),\qquad e=v_{\text{ref}}(h)-v$$
