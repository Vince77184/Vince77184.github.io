Research internship · Navier laboratory (École des Ponts, Université Gustave Eiffel, CNRS) · 2021 (2 months) · supervisor F. Rouyer

Instrumented Bench for Gas Marbles

Experimental benchHigh-speed imagingData processing

Summary

A gas marble is a bubble armoured by a layer of solid particles. It is much sturdier than a soap bubble, which makes it interesting for acoustic insulation, vibration damping or gas encapsulation — but how it forms was still poorly understood.

I built a bench where a frame is pulled out of a particle raft floating on soapy water: the granular film stretches into a catenoid, pinches and may close into a (half) gas marble. I varied frame geometry (10×10 to 50×50 mm), pulling speed (1 to 50 mm/s) and particle loading, checking each time that the process was reproducible.

On the bench

This was hands-on experimental work: designing and assembling the bench (motorised vertical stage, frames, fishing-line supports), synchronising a high-speed camera above 1000 fps because the closure lasts less than a second, then writing the Python image processing that extracts the neck radius over time, with an automatic detection of the rupture instant. Two independent series of measurements were run to check reproducibility.

Key results

  • Minimum frame size (L > 25 mm) needed to form a half gas marble
  • Pure soap films follow the τ^(2/3) retraction law; particles slow the retraction markedly
  • Retraction speed and marble diameter measured against pulling speed, reproducible over two series

Method & equations

Surface tension \(\gamma\) acts as a force per unit length and pressure jumps across a curved interface (Young–Laplace):

$$\mathrm{d}\mathbf{f}=\gamma\,\mathrm{d}\ell\,\mathbf{n},\qquad \Delta p=\gamma\left(\frac{1}{R_1}+\frac{1}{R_2}\right)$$

A soap catenoid between two rings of radius \(R\) becomes unstable when the height reaches \(h_c\approx 2R/3\). After that, the neck radius follows a power law (Chen & Steen), with \(\tau=t_{\text{rupture}}-t\):

$$r(\tau)=\left(\frac{\gamma\,\tau^{2}}{\rho}\right)^{1/3}\propto\tau^{2/3}$$

The particle-laden film is considered broken when \(r\approx(1+\tfrac{\sqrt2}{2})\,D_p\), i.e. a compact packing of particles — the switch from a continuous to a discrete description.
