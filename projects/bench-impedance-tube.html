Impedance Tube — Acoustic Absorption

ISO 10534-2Transfer-function method3 microphonesCalibration

Summary

An impedance (Kundt) tube sends a plane sound wave onto a sample of porous material — foams, wools, fibrous or granular materials — and measures how much of the energy is absorbed rather than reflected, frequency by frequency. A third microphone behind the sample gives access to the material's volume properties (equivalent density and bulk modulus), the inputs of porous-material models.

I took charge of the bench, rebuilt its configuration, and wrote a 39-page lab notebook (version 1.2): theory, hardware, microphone checks, daily calibration, sample preparation, measurement and troubleshooting.

On the bench

The method only holds if its assumptions are checked: plane waves (upper frequency set by the 35 mm microphone spacing, usable band 50–4655 Hz), inter-microphone calibration once per measurement day by swapping microphones, ambient temperature and pressure logged by a weather station (they set the speed of sound and air impedance), sample thickness measured with calipers, and sealing with PTFE tape. An α below 0 or above 1 is treated as a measurement fault, never as a result.

Key results

Method & equations

Absorption coefficient from the complex reflection coefficient \(R\) at the sample surface:

$$\alpha(\omega)=1-|R|^{2}$$

Transfer-function method (ISO 10534-2): from \(H_{12}=p(x_2)/p(x_1)\), with microphone spacing \(s\) and distance \(L\) to the sample (\(q_0=\omega/c_0\)):

$$R=e^{2jq_0L}\,\frac{H_{12}-e^{-jq_0s}}{e^{jq_0s}-H_{12}}$$

With the third microphone (\(H_{23}\)), the wave number \(q_c\) and characteristic impedance \(Z_c\) of the material give the equivalent-fluid properties:

$$\rho_{eq}=\frac{Z_c\,q_c}{\omega},\qquad K_{eq}=\frac{\omega\,Z_c}{q_c}$$

Validity of plane waves for a tube of diameter \(D\): \(f_u<0.58\,c_0/D\), \(s<0.45\,c_0/f_u\), \(s>0.05\,c_0/f_l\).
