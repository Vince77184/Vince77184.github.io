MSc thesis · University of California, Santa Barbara · 2024 (6 months)

Flows in Reactive Porous Media

Numerical modelValidation on benchmarkPublished

Summary

When minerals precipitate or dissolve inside a porous rock or filter, the pores themselves change shape — and with them permeability and the way fluid flows. This matters for carbon sequestration, geothermal energy, water filtration and catalysis. During my MSc thesis in Prof. Frédéric Gibou's group at UC Santa Barbara, I worked on a numerical method that follows this evolution with a sharp solid–liquid interface, on adaptive quadtree grids that refine only where needed.

The work became a first-author paper: A Sharp Numerical Approach for Modeling Flows in Reactive Porous Media, V. Poitier, E. Bayat, K. Hernandez Leyva, A. Sauret, F. Gibou — Journal of Computational Physics, 2026.

On the bench

The method was first validated against a known benchmark (dissolution of a calcite grain, compared with several published codes), then checked for grid convergence on the merging of two growing grains. I defined these test protocols, compared the model with reference data and automated all post-processing in Python and C/C++ — the same reflex I now apply to physical test benches: no result is trusted before it is checked against a reference.

Key results

  • Sharp interface on adaptive grids: about 100,000 grid points instead of a uniform fine mesh
  • Validated on a published dissolution benchmark and with grid-convergence studies
  • Captures effective permeability, tortuosity and pore-scale velocity profiles as the pores close

Method & equations

Fluid flow in the pores: incompressible Navier–Stokes,

$$\rho_l\left(\frac{\partial \mathbf{v}}{\partial t}+\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{v}\right)=-\nabla p+\mu_l\,\Delta\mathbf{v},\qquad \nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}=0 \quad\text{in }\Omega_l$$

Transport of the dissolved species (advection–diffusion):

$$\frac{\partial C}{\partial t}+\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla C=D_l\,\Delta C$$

At the moving solid–liquid interface \(\Gamma(t)\), mass balance gives a Stefan condition for the interface velocity, and the interface is tracked with a level-set function \(\phi\):

$$\mathbf{v}_\Gamma\cdot\mathbf{n}\approx-D_l\,\nabla C\cdot\mathbf{n},\qquad \frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}+\mathbf{v}_\Gamma\cdot\nabla\phi=0$$

Regimes are explored through the Péclet, Damköhler and Reynolds numbers, \(\mathrm{Pe}=UL/D_l\), \(\mathrm{Da}=kL/D_l\), \(\mathrm{Re}=\rho_l UL/\mu_l\).
