Static Airflow Resistivity Bench

ISO 9053-1Darcy's lawAcoustiFlowTwo-face measurement

Summary

A slow, perfectly controlled laminar airflow crosses the sample and the pressure drop between its two faces is measured: the more the material "brakes" the air, the higher its resistivity σ. Together with open porosity, σ is one of the two first-order inputs needed to identify porous-material models from impedance-tube measurements.

I wrote the 36-page lab notebook (version 2.1): theory, the AcoustiFlow resistivity meter and its compressor, the two sample geometries, the procedure (face A, then face B), result checks and how the value feeds the RoKCell characterisation software.

On the bench

The notebook insists on what makes the number trustworthy: staying in the Darcy regime (pressure drop proportional to speed), measuring at several speeds and bringing the result back to the 0.5 mm/s reference of the standard, logging temperature and pressure (air viscosity changes by about 0.3 % per °C), measuring both faces of every sample and cross-checking σ with the estimate from the acoustic measurements.

Key results

  • Procedure compliant with ISO 9053-1 static method, two faces per sample
  • Temperature/pressure logging and conversion to intrinsic permeability k₀
  • 36-page lab notebook (v2.1) with troubleshooting and session sheet

Method & equations

ISO 9053-1 defines three nested quantities for a sample of thickness \(e\), section \(S\), crossed by a flow rate \(Q\) (speed \(v=Q/S\)) with pressure drop \(\Delta P\):

$$R=\frac{\Delta P}{Q},\qquad R_s=R\,S=\frac{\Delta P}{v},\qquad \sigma=\frac{R_s}{e}=\frac{\Delta P}{v\,e}\quad[\mathrm{N\,s\,m^{-4}}]$$

At very low speed the flow obeys Darcy's law; σ is linked to the viscous static permeability \(k_0\), the truly intrinsic quantity (\(\eta\approx1.8\times10^{-5}\) Pa·s for air):

$$\mathbf{v}=-\frac{k_0}{\eta}\,\nabla P\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\frac{\Delta P}{e}=\sigma\,v,\qquad \sigma=\frac{\eta}{k_0}$$
